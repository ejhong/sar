01 / THE MECHANISM

How surface spacing
becomes model depth.

We reconstructed two focusing variants from the published descriptions. In the static examples, changes in the surface interference pattern become patch shifts; oscillations in those shifts become peaks on a depth-labelled axis.

  1. 01

    Start with a complex image

    The simulated radar image records amplitude and phase from a known stationary scene.

  2. 02

    Slice the Doppler spectrum

    Sweep 50 overlapping reference/offset sub-aperture pairs across the processed band.

  3. 03

    Measure patch shifts

    Cross-correlate 32 × 32 pixel patches. Each target gets a sequence of 50 displacement vectors.

  4. 04

    Focus along a model axis

    Project or fit oscillations in that sequence using depth-dependent steering vectors.

For this straight-line model, Doppler frequency maps to an effective slow time and along-track platform offset. Image columns themselves are not a raw time series. The implemented steering wavenumber is Kz = 4πB / (λsR sin θ), with B taken from that offset. Substitution gives z = Δx λs sin θ / λ for the ideal interfering-pair example.

The paper-style output is |a(z)ᴴY|²/K². The window-fit output takes the maximum adjusted R² across 25-sample windows. It is a fit score, not a probability of geology; maximizing over depths and windows increases opportunities to fit incidental structure.

04 / WHAT WOULD CHANGE THE EVIDENCE

Build the missing links.
Test on ground truth.

A useful next question is whether radar measurements add information about surveyed cavities beyond what the visible surface and geology already predict.

01

Repeat the reconstruction on real scenes

Use documented complex acquisitions and matched pyramid, plateau and urban controls. Check registration, processing assumptions, the full selection gate and false alarms before interpreting a volume.

Planned · real data required
02

Connect a cavity to a radar measurement

Calibrate the source, material properties and surface displacement. Carry that motion through acquisition and focusing, then compare a phase-sensitive estimator with the patch-shift pipeline under the same noise and clutter.

Open · forward models are uncoupled
03

Use independently surveyed positives and negatives

Start with several simpler surveyed quarry or tunnel sites. Match intact controls by geology and surface conditions. An unmapped area is unknown, not a confirmed negative. Reserve entire sites for the final test.

Planned · surveys and matched controls
04

Measure added information

Compare surface-only, radar-amplitude, complex and repeated-acquisition features. Mask entrances and infrastructure, use spatial placebo labels, report false alarms per area, and test detection, depth and shape separately.

Planned · evaluation fixed in advance

Repeated-acquisition deformation monitoring is also a distinct route worth evaluating. A stable old cavity need not produce measurable deformation. A high classifier score, a visual match, and a measured subsurface location are different levels of evidence.

A short radar glossary +
SAR
Synthetic aperture radar: a moving radar combines echoes to form an image.
SLC
A single-look complex image, retaining amplitude and phase.
Sub-aperture
A subset of the acquisition's aperture or processed Doppler spectrum.
Layover
Different heights and ground locations can occupy the same radar-image pixels.
Model depth
The coordinate assigned by the steering model. It needs independent calibration and validation.
Adjusted R²
A measure of fit to a selected oscillation model. It is not a detection probability.
Aliasing
Different oscillation rates can become indistinguishable after sampling.
Held-out site
An entire physical location reserved for evaluation, with none of its patches used for training.
