A simulation lab for the underworld

What can see beneath the ground, and how far down

In March 2025 a team announced an underground city beneath Khafre's pyramid, mapped from satellite radar alone. The published method behind it has since been retracted. Katabasis settles the question the way physics can: build ground whose contents are known, simulate the instruments over it, and measure what each one recovers. First the seismic arrays geophysicists trust, then the satellite.

A site as data: terrain, strata, water table, heterogeneity, and chambers cut into it. Every material property is sourced or marked assumed.

Full three-dimensional elastic waves through that ground, recorded by the instruments real surveys use: geophone arrays now, the ICEYE dwell next.

Invert the records the way practitioners do, score the tomogram against the truth, and sweep size, depth and array until each method fails.

Seismic tomography reaches the chambers it is designed for

The reference ceiling. Hammer shots and ambient noise recorded on dense geophone arrays over each test bench, inverted by travel-time tomography, ambient-noise tomography and full-waveform inversion. What the best ground instruments recover is the most any satellite could hope to match.
