Can radar reveal
what lies below?

Measuring surface motion, locating a cavity and reconstructing columns are three different claims.

The proposed route infers underground geometry from surface motion. Known physical inputs, noise controls and competing explanations test when depth is recoverable and when an image can mislead.

Where the evidence stands
  1. Surface motion: measurable.

    Demonstrated on controlled satellite targets in published work; verified in our ideal measurement model.

  2. Depth: possible under tested assumptions.

    Our coupled model succeeds with sufficient signal and known excitation. Wrong assumptions can produce false detections.

  3. Khafre columns: not established here.

    Original scans are available for inspection. No buried structure or depth has been validated with them in this notebook.

01 / CAN THE SURFACE MOTION BE MEASURED?

Start with a signal
we know is there.

Controlled experiments have already shown satellite SAR measuring surface vibration. The next question is whether a particular acquisition and processor recover the motion needed for an underground inference.

External measured evidence

A 2026 study compared satellite SAR with simultaneous ground measurements on vibrating targets. It recovered 1–4 Hz motion with radial RMS amplitudes from 10.43 to 0.10 mm. These were controlled, highly reflective targets. Vattulainen et al., IEEE Access ↗

Our positive control preserves the motion.

A known displacement changes the radar phase, passes through a simplified focusing operation, and is recovered from the complex image. The ideal pulse intensity does not change. A failed patch-shift test therefore cannot rule out every way to measure vibration.

What this establishes

The phase channel and its units work in our controlled acquisition. Known reflector positions, independent receiver noise and perfect focusing are favorable assumptions. This is not a field-validated extraction method for distributed stone or soil.

Conditional feasibility, with clear limits

Depth information survives in this simple model when motion and measurement precision are sufficient and the physical assumptions are appropriate. The supplied amplitudes and SNRs are not measurements at Giza. The model contains a horizontal cylindrical void of 20 m radius, known active excitation and uniform rock. It does not test vertical columns, spiral ramps, kilometre-scale depth or ambient-source recovery.

03 / IS DEPTH THE ONLY EXPLANATION?

Test the alternatives
that can look convincing.

A repeatable feature can still come from surface scattering or an incorrect source model. These tests change the possible explanation while holding other parts of the experiment fixed.

Physical source × physical depth

Does the processing follow the buried object?

We independently vary cavity depth and excitation frequency, then run the coupled radar images through our patch-shift reconstruction. The model wavelength stays fixed. Its full filter accepts 0 of 63 reflector observations from nine moving scenes.

What this means

This reconstruction has not demonstrated sensitivity to these physical cavity signals. The ungated peaks are diagnostic outputs only. These SH waves have one displacement polarization; the ellipse filter demands a two-axis registration trajectory. Its rejection of these inputs does not establish sensitivity to richer elastic motion. Rejecting static controls alone does not establish a useful cavity detector.

Unknown excitation

Could a different source imitate the cavity?

At one frequency, seven unconstrained surface sources in cavity-free rock can reproduce the seven measured complex displacements from a cavity. At fourteen additional receivers, mismatch is 2.7% at 8 Hz and 40.3% at 25 Hz. Extra measurements can expose the alternative.

A limited non-uniqueness result

The fitted source weights are free to vary in amplitude and phase. This does not establish that such sources exist at Khafre. It shows why source constraints, multiple frequencies and independent locations matter before a sparse vibration pattern is assigned a unique underground shape.

04 / CAN SURFACE EFFECTS PRODUCE THE PATTERN?

Test artifacts.
Keep the full decision rule.

Surface interference can produce oscillating image-registration vectors and depth-like output in our reconstruction. An ungated pattern and a validated underground detection are different outcomes.

Independent static scenes, with the full rule.

The corrected per-window filter accepts no target in 32 independent stationary scenes (0 scenes with a pass). It also rejects the moving-reflector examples above. These two outcomes must be read together.

Each class has only 16 independent images; zero passing scenes gives a 95% Wilson upper bound of about 19% for that scene-level rate. This is not a zero false-alarm guarantee. Original T1–T7 images are additional mechanism and sensitivity studies, not independent field trials.

Which method is actually being tested? +

The original publications, the public derivative protocol and this implementation are separate objects of study. The derivative protocol explicitly supplies choices absent from the original descriptions. These experiments cannot be called an exact replication of the original Khafre processing.

03MechanismSurface spacing predicts the peak+

Surface spacing predicts the apparent depth

What exactly does the steering matrix respond to, if not motion?

In the two-scatterer examples, peak depth tracks azimuth separation at approximately 8.86 model-metres per metre. Farther scatterers can contribute outside the patch, and the sampled depth axis folds high spatial frequencies back by aliasing.

What this leaves open

Ideal point scatterers and a prescribed spectral model. The wall's comb fit depends on reflectivity: the ideal-wall fitted spacing differs from the prediction, while the randomized variants are closer. These examples identify a mechanism in this reconstruction, not the cause of a particular published feature.

Processing choices set the axis and change the patterns

Do the 'depths' survive changes that a physical measurement would ignore?

One scene per sweep, with one parameter changed at a time. Sensitivity alone does not invalidate an inverse method; the relevant question is whether calibrated, independently validated targets remain correctly localized. No such validation is supplied by this experiment.

05Positive inputMotion enters; frequency becomes depth+

Motion changes the output; frequency becomes model depth

Grant the premise: if the ground really vibrated, would the method see it, and would it locate it?

At 1 cm imposed amplitude, the response reaches 3.58 times the static trajectory rms. The focusing step places vibration near 10.87 model-metres per hertz; the 2 Hz case peaks at a second harmonic. This is a response measurement, not a calibrated detection limit.

What this leaves open

One sinusoidal patch and static background clutter. Subtracting the exactly known motion-free baseline is an advantage available in simulation. No sensor-noise distribution, false-alarm calibration, or cavity-to-surface model is included. The result does not establish a general limit on radar vibrometry.

06Selection gatesThe full gate rejects these static pixels+

The full ellipse gate rejects the sampled static pixels

Does motionless ground pass the complete per-window ellipse filter, including its amplitude floor?

The corrected per-window shape gate accepts 14.3% of pyramid and 12.9% of desert pixels. After the 0.005 px minor-axis floor, the full gate accepts 0.0% and 0.0%. The earlier shuffle comparison is retained as a diagnostic only.

What this leaves open

One scene per class and 1,500 dependent pixels. Arbitrary look permutations change correlations between overlapping sub-apertures, so the shuffle statistic is not a calibrated physical null test. The new independent-scene controls replace it as the primary null comparison. Shape-only acceptance is not a false positive of the full gate; sensitivity must also be tested on physical positives.

Method, numbers & reproducibility +

For each of 26 contiguous W25 windows, choose the best ellipse mode from 1–6, then apply adjusted R² ≥ 0.25, axis ratio ≥ 0.1 and minor axis ≥ 0.005 px. Pixel acceptance requires at least one fully accepted window. Legacy shuffle results use 60 arbitrary order permutations and are descriptive only.

One background realization, three random perimeter layouts and ideal point targets with prescribed amplitudes. Added points have no terrain-shadowing or object-specific scattering model. Why accurate placement comes before a depth test +

At the Giza scene centre, changing assumed surface elevation by 10 m shifts the metadata projection by about 61 range pixels, equivalent to roughly 26 m on flat ground. This is a geometric sensitivity calculation, not measured surface motion. It is large compared with the candidate chambers.

The HDF5 raster is 113,692 × 11,562 pixels; the companion SICD XML describes 11,558 × 113,620. A transpose cannot register the two products. The native RPC projection also has residual differences from the supplied centre and corner coordinates when one average scene height is used. Those metadata points are not independent surveyed landmarks.

Next: align survey plans with independent surface landmarks and their elevations, record the remaining placement uncertainty, and verify the aperture support needed for motion analysis. No target-specific adjustment will be fitted to make a depth result match a tomb.

Large files: what is needed and how to inspect them +

Keep files in place. The inspector reads metadata without loading the raster. An optional explicit crop has a working-memory cap. HDF5 I/Q and uncompressed complex TIFF reads are supported; SICD/NITF uses optional sarpy. Compressed TIFFs need a windowed reader.

.venv/bin/python scripts/inspect_sar.py /path/to/scene.h5

# Optional native-image QC crop; select bounds explicitly.
.venv/bin/python scripts/inspect_sar.py /path/to/scene.h5 \
  --rows 1000 1512 --cols 2000 2512 --out data/inspection

A spatial crop can truncate the support needed for sub-aperture analysis. This preview is for image and metadata QC; the processing region is chosen after inspecting the acquisition. Missing geometry is reported rather than filled with simulated defaults.

Umbra’s open-data program also supplies complex products. Available imagery alone does not supply surveyed cavity labels.

A short glossary +
SAR / SLC
A moving radar forms an image; a single-look complex image retains amplitude and phase.
Surface motion
A displacement that changes radar phase or image behavior. Its underground cause is a separate inference.
Model depth
A coordinate assigned by a model. Ground truth is needed to validate it.
Identifiability
Whether different physical explanations can be distinguished by the measurements.
Positive control
An input containing a known signal the method should recover.
False alarm
An accepted detection on a known negative under the complete, fixed rule.
SNR
Signal-to-noise ratio. Here it is prescribed per isolated reflector and aperture sample.
Held-out depth
A target depth excluded from the estimator’s candidate library.
Layover
Different elevations and locations overlap in radar-image coordinates.
