01 / THE MECHANISM

How surface spacing
becomes model depth.

We reconstructed two focusing variants from the published descriptions. In the static examples, changes in the surface interference pattern become patch shifts; oscillations in those shifts become peaks on a depth-labelled axis.

  1. 01

    Start with a complex image

    The simulated radar image records amplitude and phase from a known stationary scene.

  2. 02

    Slice the Doppler spectrum

    Sweep 50 overlapping reference/offset sub-aperture pairs across the processed band.

  3. 03

    Measure patch shifts

    Cross-correlate 32 × 32 pixel patches. Each target gets a sequence of 50 displacement vectors.

  4. 04

    Focus along a model axis

    Project or fit oscillations in that sequence using depth-dependent steering vectors.

Read the equations and processing settings +

For this straight-line model, Doppler frequency maps to an effective slow time and along-track platform offset. Image columns themselves are not a raw time series. The implemented steering wavenumber is Kz = 4πB / (λsR sin θ), with B taken from that offset. Substitution gives z = Δx λs sin θ / λ for the ideal interfering-pair example.

The paper-style output is |a(z)ᴴY|²/K². The window-fit output takes the maximum adjusted R² across 25-sample windows. It is a fit score, not a probability of geology; maximizing over depths and windows increases opportunities to fit incidental structure.

01Static scenePatterns without buried objects+

A stationary pyramid produces depth-like patterns

If nothing moves and nothing is buried, does the method still draw structures at depth?

The reconstructed pipeline produces layered patterns although the simulated scene has no motion or subsurface. Mean best window-fit scores are 0.306 inside the ground footprint and 0.308 outside it.

What this leaves open

One stylized scene and one random seed. The footprint is a ground-plane mask, not a scatterer attribution map: layover mixes locations. A high fit score is not a probability of a buried structure.

02Null controlDesert and pyramid scores overlap+

Empty desert produces a similar score distribution

Is the output any different when there is no pyramid at all?

The mean best score is 0.306 on the pyramid footprint and 0.306 in the desert-only scene. Their empirical distribution distance is 0.0096. The absence of buried objects does not prevent structured output.

What this leaves open

One realization per scene; the 32-pixel patches overlap on a 4-pixel grid, so the samples are dependent. The saved conventional KS p-value is descriptive only here. Similar distributions do not establish statistical equivalence or validate a detector.

03MechanismSurface spacing predicts the peak+

Surface spacing predicts the apparent depth

What exactly does the steering matrix respond to, if not motion?

In the two-scatterer examples, peak depth tracks azimuth separation at approximately 8.86 model-metres per metre. Farther scatterers can contribute outside the patch, and the sampled depth axis folds high spatial frequencies back by aliasing.

What this leaves open

Ideal point scatterers and a prescribed spectral model. The wall's comb fit depends on reflectivity: the ideal-wall fitted spacing differs from the prediction, while the randomized variants are closer. These examples identify a mechanism in this reconstruction, not the cause of a particular published feature.

04ParametersThe depth scale is a processing choice+

Processing choices set the axis and change the patterns

Do the 'depths' survive changes that a physical measurement would ignore?

Patch size changes spatial smoothness. The declared wavelength scales the model-depth axis, and the sub-aperture width changes its sampling and the resulting pattern. The wavelength panel illustrates an exact algebraic rescaling; a separate end-to-end test verifies that relation.

What this leaves open

One scene per sweep, with one parameter changed at a time. Sensitivity alone does not invalidate an inverse method; the relevant question is whether calibrated, independently validated targets remain correctly localized. No such validation is supplied by this experiment.

05Positive inputMotion enters; frequency becomes depth+

Motion changes the output; frequency becomes model depth

Grant the premise: if the ground really vibrated, would the method see it, and would it locate it?

At 1 cm imposed amplitude, the response reaches 3.58 times the static trajectory rms. The focusing step places vibration near 10.87 model-metres per hertz; the 2 Hz case peaks at a second harmonic. This is a response measurement, not a calibrated detection limit.

What this leaves open

One sinusoidal patch and static background clutter. Subtracting the exactly known motion-free baseline is an advantage available in simulation. No sensor-noise distribution, false-alarm calibration, or cavity-to-surface model is included. The result does not establish a general limit on radar vibrometry.

Method, numbers & reproducibility +

Static desert SLC plus a synthesised vibrating patch (linear superposition), 5 frequencies × 7 amplitudes from 1 µm to 1 cm; response measured as the change of the sampled patch trajectories relative to the same patch at rest, compared with the rms of the static trajectories.

06Selection gatesThe full gate rejects these static pixels+

The full ellipse gate rejects the sampled static pixels

The replication protocol keeps only pixels whose trajectories trace ordered elliptical loops. Does motionless ground pass, and is there any ordering to find?

The shape-only gate accepts 9.9% of pyramid and 9.1% of desert pixels. After the 0.005 px minor-axis floor, the full gate accepts 0.0% and 0.0%. True-order scores show near-nominal permutation rejection rates in these scenes.

What this leaves open

One scene realization per class, 1,500 dependent pixels, and 60 permutations. A near-chance rejection rate is not proof that all ordering is absent. Shape-only acceptance must not be described as a false positive of the full gate.

Method, numbers & reproducibility +

Per-pixel best score in true order versus 60 random orderings, plus the protocol's branch-B ellipse fit (26 windows × modes 1 to 6, adjusted R² with 6 parameters, axes from the fitted cos/sin vectors) on 1,500 pixels on the pyramid footprint (T1) and 1,500 on the empty desert (T2).

07RenderingSurface features become columns+

Surface features produce columns with a shared display floor

Can the specific published imagery, vertical wells with rungs descending to a common floor, come out of a scene with nothing underneath?

In this constructed scene, dominant point scatterers produce dark columns (0.20 times desert power), while mixed face edges produce bright bands (4.84 times desert power). The displayed volume ends at 137.5 model-metres, or 630.4 m after a chosen wavelength rescaling.

What this leaves open

Bright points are placed by hand, and smoothing and display thresholds are chosen for illustration. Visual resemblance cannot establish how the published images were produced or identify the cause of their features. The simulation neither reproduces nor measures spiral ramps, and its rung spacings do not cleanly follow a single course period.

Additional checksDo the examples survive simple stress tests?+

Across 40 two-scatterer runs, median peak error relative to the spacing prediction is 0.92 m; the largest is 2.25 m. End-to-end reruns at three wavelengths verify the predicted rescaling.

The permutation test flags 100.0% of planted ordered trajectories and 2.1% of independent-noise trajectories, at a nominal 5% level. It is sensitive to this positive control, which is generated in trajectory space rather than through a radar simulation.

03 / SIGNAL & INFERENCE

A measurable signal is
only the first step.

Could underground geometry affect surface motion in a way radar can use? These supporting experiments examine separate parts of that chain.

  1. 01Underground geometryCavity, material, source
  2. 02Surface motionAmplitude and wave pattern
  3. 03Radar measurementPhase, noise, processing
  4. 04Validated inferenceDetection, depth, geometry

The wave and receiver models below are independent. No calibrated cavity-to-radar simulation or field detection is demonstrated here.

S1 / What is measured

Phase and translation answer different questions.

A correlation peak tells us how far a patch shifts. A phase measurement can respond while that shift remains zero. This explains why T5's response cannot be treated as a universal limit on radar vibration measurements.

The control applies a phase rotation equivalent to a ±30 μm displacement. Both estimators receive exactly the same patch.

How sensitive is an ideal phase receiver? +

With a 3.1 cm wavelength, 8 s record, 512 pulses/s and 20 dB per-pulse signal-to-noise ratio, the phase detector finds the 30 μm input in 99.9% of 800 trials. Independent zero-signal trials produce 1.12% detections against a nominal 1% threshold. Distributed clutter, autofocus, atmospheric effects and real acquisition geometry are absent.

Airborne vibration measurements provide a precedent for controlled target vibrometry; they do not validate satellite cavity mapping. Wang et al., 2012 ↗

S2 / The physical connection

A cavity can change the surface wavefield.

A simple shear-wave model gives different surface responses with and without a buried cavity. The location and timing of the difference change with cavity depth.

The model represents an infinitely long cylindrical cavity with radius 20 m. It has a known active source, uniform rock and no intrinsic attenuation. Its displacement amplitude is uncalibrated.

Numerical check: halving grid spacing gives a cavity-perturbation correlation of 0.954; the relative change at 120 m shifts from 24.5% to 23.3%. This supports the qualitative model effect, not a depth limit.

S3 / Learning from the right evidence

A model can recognize a site without detecting a cavity.

In the site-only control, randomly split patches score AUC 1.00. Holding out whole sites reduces it to 0.51, near chance. No transferable cavity signal was supplied.

A second control deliberately supplies a transferable feature and gets better held-out performance. Test for added information beyond location, geology and visible surface clues.

04 / WHAT WOULD CHANGE THE EVIDENCE

Build the missing links.
Test on ground truth.

A useful next question is whether radar measurements add information about surveyed cavities beyond what the visible surface and geology already predict.

01

Repeat the reconstruction on real scenes

Use documented complex acquisitions and matched pyramid, plateau and urban controls. Check registration, processing assumptions, the full selection gate and false alarms before interpreting a volume.

Planned · real data required
02

Connect a cavity to a radar measurement

Calibrate the source, material properties and surface displacement. Carry that motion through acquisition and focusing, then compare a phase-sensitive estimator with the patch-shift pipeline under the same noise and clutter.

Open · forward models are uncoupled
03

Use independently surveyed positives and negatives

Start with several simpler surveyed quarry or tunnel sites. Match intact controls by geology and surface conditions. An unmapped area is unknown, not a confirmed negative. Reserve entire sites for the final test.

Planned · surveys and matched controls
04

Measure added information

Compare surface-only, radar-amplitude, complex and repeated-acquisition features. Mask entrances and infrastructure, use spatial placebo labels, report false alarms per area, and test detection, depth and shape separately.

Planned · evaluation fixed in advance

Repeated-acquisition deformation monitoring is also a distinct route worth evaluating. A stable old cavity need not produce measurable deformation. A high classifier score, a visual match, and a measured subsurface location are different levels of evidence.

A short radar glossary +
SAR
Synthetic aperture radar: a moving radar combines echoes to form an image.
SLC
A single-look complex image, retaining amplitude and phase.
Sub-aperture
A subset of the acquisition's aperture or processed Doppler spectrum.
Layover
Different heights and ground locations can occupy the same radar-image pixels.
Model depth
The coordinate assigned by the steering model. It needs independent calibration and validation.
Adjusted R²
A measure of fit to a selected oscillation model. It is not a detection probability.
Aliasing
Different oscillation rates can become indistinguishable after sampling.
Held-out site
An entire physical location reserved for evaluation, with none of its patches used for training.
