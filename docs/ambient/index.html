Ambient · measured

The ground's own motion

Without a hammer the ground still moves: ocean swell felt as microseisms, wind, traffic, footsteps. It is what ambient-noise seismology listens to and what the radar claim says it reads. How much it moves near Giza, and at what frequencies, sets what either can see. Here it is measured, not assumed.

Near Giza the ground moves by hundredths of a micrometre per second on its own

The nearest open broadband record is MedNet station KEG at Kottamya, 67 km east of Khafre: an STS-1 seismometer in a 7 m vault on a limestone hill. Twelve days of 1996, one in the middle of each month, three components, instrument response removed, turned into power spectra the way network operators do. Giza’s ground rings near 0.6 Hz, and the Great Pyramid near 2.3 Hz

An ambient-noise survey of Khufu’s pyramid, 37 points in its chambers, on its blocks and on the ground around it (ELGabry and others, Scientific Reports 16, 14032, 2026), found the pyramid’s fundamental frequency between 2.0 and 2.6 Hz, “with an average of ~ 2.3”, and “the average predominant frequency on the surrounding ground is about 0.60 Hz”. The survey reports spectral ratios, not absolute levels. We have found no open, absolute measurement of ambient motion on the plateau itself; the city’s traffic will raise it above Kottamya’s above about 1 Hz, by an amount still to be sourced.

The ground’s own waves are kilometres long where they are strong, and metres long only where they are local

What each band can see

In the microseism band, where the ground moves most, a wave is 6 to 17 km long: it feels the average of kilometres of rock. At Giza’s 0.6 Hz resonance it is about 3 km. To resolve a 6 m chamber takes about 141 Hz; a 10 m shaft about 85 Hz.

At those frequencies there is no regional noise field, only sources within tens of metres: footsteps, engines, pumps. That is the setting of the ambient-noise imaging test, which even then, with the rock known exactly and an unlimited record, reaches only 2.2× at the chamber (P1-04).

The radar’s floor is 22× above the noisiest microseisms and about 1,600× above Giza’s

On the real ICEYE dwell over Giza, the line-of-sight velocity floor on the brightest targets is 77 µm/s, the same on monuments as on open desert (R4). The microseisms near Giza move the ground 0.049 µm/s; even Peterson’s high-noise model, the world’s noisiest stations, reaches 3.5 µm/s. The first investigation set ambient motion at 0.1–10 µm/s, an unsourced range that made the shortfall look like 8×; measured, it is at least 22×.

Next: the ambient field as part of every site

Microseisms

Surface waves from distant storms, arriving from every direction as plane waves at 0.1–1 Hz, their levels from this measurement. Too long for the 3-D grid; computed exactly for a layered ground.

Cultural noise

Traffic on the roads round the plateau, visitors on its paths, the city beyond: sources with measured spectra, simulated on coarser grids over hundreds of metres. Their absolute level at Giza is the open question.

One field, three uses

The ambient-noise test redone at realistic levels; the noise on the active surveys, replacing white noise; and the ground motion the simulated ICEYE dwell looks at in Phase 2.
