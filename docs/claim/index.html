What was published, and by whom

The claim under test

The idea is that a single satellite radar image, reprocessed as a set of sub-apertures, holds the vibration of the ground, and that the vibration can be focused into a picture of what lies hundreds of metres beneath it. This page keeps apart the four things that are easily blurred: the published claims, the public protocol derived from them, this lab's own work, and the difference between simulation and measurement.

The method was published in 2022 and retracted in 2026

claimed

Single-image Doppler tomographyDoppler tomography radarThe claimed method: reading ground vibration from the differences between sub-apertures of one image and focusing it into depth. Published 2022, retracted 2026.

Biondi and Malanga described the method in 2022, applied to the Great Pyramid (arXiv:2208.00811). The paper was retracted by Remote Sensing on 10 August 2026 (retraction notice).

claimed

The Khafre announcement

At a briefing in Bologna on 15 March 2025, the Khafre Project described eight vertical structures reaching more than 2,000 feet beneath the pyramid and further structures near 4,000 feet. The claim has not appeared in a peer-reviewed paper. Its geometry, as far as it was stated, is placed in the ground inthe claimed underworld.

Replications reconstruct the method, with choices of their own

Since 2025 the method has been reconstructed from its paper by others, filling missing details with choices of their own. This lab tests the method as published and the general idea behind it, and keeps any reconstruction's choices apart from the original's. Where a reconstruction adds stricter selection before its depth focus, the lab runs it unchanged beside controls: motionless copies of the image pass its gates too, and planted vibrations do not come back.
