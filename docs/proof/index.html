Proof · what any way of reading one image could learn, and what it assumes

The bound, finite and stated

Can one satellite radar image reveal what is underground (whether something is there, where it is, its shape) even with its depth uncertain? Within a stated model this can be bounded for every algorithm at once. One auditable comparison, five worlds identical but for the cavity, shows how far: under the ground’s own trembling or beside a bouncing lorry, no method reading one image comes near reliable detection; nor, computed, of the structures claimed under Khafre. The model, its inputs and what is assumed are set out below; the physical inputs are models and are marked as such.

what it rests on

Computed, measured, specified, assumed

Computed: each world’s motion by the lab’s elastic solver within 70 m; the per-line certificate, checked against the exact divergence on lines (0.18 of it at most); the oracle’s formula pulse by pulse on fixed scenes (0.994 ± 0.006, unchanged with the lorry’s wave ten times larger).

Measured in the image, over a specified floor: the ground’s backscatter is measured; the noise is not (neither product carries a floor), so the signal-to-noise ratio takes ICEYE’s best specified figure for the product’s mode, 18.4 dB, the most favourable to a detector; the image’s darkest cells bound it only from below (5.9 dB).

Assumed: the scattered wave beyond 70 m, carried undiminished to the image’s edge (98% of the lorry case’s signal; measured falling as r-0.4 out to 70 m; five times larger, still at most 31%); the lorry’s level held all pass; one geology; fully developed speckle (bright points are covered by the oracle only).

Achieved: the score test, told the pattern, on synthesised images with one noise law, its threshold from 400 calibration grounds and read on 100 + 100 others: with the difference amplified 800 times it finds 73% (95% interval 63–81%) at 15% false alarms, AUC 0.91 (predicted 0.93). The threshold targeted 5% false alarms but gave 15% (9–24%) on the held-out grounds, which remains unexplained: the discrimination is supported by the AUC, calibration at a 5% operating point is not established. At the real level 7% at 15% false alarms (p 0.98), AUC 0.47: at chance.

Why it holds: six finite steps, none assuming a resonance peak or a particular algorithm

A · every processor

Processing cannot add distinguishability

For any test, detection rate − false-alarm rate ≤ TV(P₁, P₀), and for equal priors the best accuracy is ½ + TV/2. Any channel applied the same way under both hypotheses, a sub-aperture bank, a tomogram, a renderer, a trained network, contracts TV. So 95% found at 5% false alarms needs TV ≥ 0.9, and a proved TV below it excludes that target for every method at once.

Proof

Split p₁ − p₀ into positive and negative parts of equal mass TV. A test 0 ≤ t ≤ 1 integrates to at most TV against it; the indicator of the positive part attains it. A processor K pulls back to the test y ↦ E[t(K(y))], so it can do no better on K’s output.

B · the finite change

The exact Gaussian divergence, bounded

For proper complex Gaussians, KL = tr(C₀⁻¹C₁) − d − log det(C₀⁻¹C₁) + δm*C₀⁻¹δm. With E the whitened covariance change and its eigenvalues ej > −1, KL = Σ(ej − log(1 + ej)) + δm*C₀⁻¹δm, and since e − log(1 + e) ≤ e²/(2 min(1 + e, 1)), for equal means (speckle is zero-mean; otherwise the mean term is added) and ‖E‖ < 1, KL ≤ ‖E‖F²/(2(1 − ‖E‖)). Pinsker and Bretagnolle–Huber turn KL into TV. No expansion in the displacement is made.

C · a certificate from a phase envelope

The whole image given the room’s largest phase

If ‖C₀−½ΔA‖F ≤ q, ρ = 2q + q² bounds ‖E‖ exactly (E = DB* + BD* + DD*), so KL ≤ ρ²/(2(1 − ρ)) and TV ≤ min(√(KL/2), √(1 − e−KL)). A phase-only change obeys |e−iΦ − 1|² ≤ Φ², so q² ≤ Σ|T₀|²Φ² / λmin(C₀) ≤ m P²/λmin: the peak phase P at every one of the image’s 1.31 billion pixels, over its 703 million retained bins. A background motion bounded the same way proves λmin(C₀) ≥ [max(0, 1 − √Qbg)]² (plus the receiver noise’s variance, where it is counted) = 0.0559.

C′ · one line at a time

A valid reference under any shaking

Widen the image’s range band to every bin (the real image is a function of the widened one, so this can only add information): each along-track line is then independent, and KL adds over lines. On each line, C₀ = B₀B₀* + σ²I with B₀ = E∘e−iφ₀, E orthonormal, so λmin(C₀) ≥ σ² + [max(0, 1 − ‖B₀ − E‖F)]² (where the motion’s term fails, the receiver noise alone still floors it) with that line’s own motion in B₀: the lorry’s wave, the ground’s trembling at its worst realisation, world 0’s own cavity. The whole image’s floor failed under the lorry (its common phase energy over 1.3 billion pixels exceeds one); one line’s is at least 0.90. Theorem C on each line, summed, bounds every reader of the one image. Tested against the exact divergence on random lines and on the benchmark’s own (0.18 of the certificate).

D · the oracle

Told everything but the answer

Given the reflectivity, the background motion, the shaking and both models, the raw echoes are Gaussian with the receiver noise’s covariance and means that differ by the echo of the room-specific motion alone: TV = erf(√d/2) exactly, d the noise-normalised energy of that difference. Averaged over what the oracle was told (only independent uniform phases needed), TV ≤ erf(√(E d)/2); the background motion cancels; 95% at 5% needs E d ≥ 5.41. Checked against pulse-by-pulse echoes on the real dwell: 0.993 ± 0.025 of the formula. The bound is on the average over scenes; for one scene, by Markov’s inequality, d ≤ t E d with probability at least 1 − 1/t (the spread measured on fixed scenes is a few per cent, P2-29, P2-36). Every realisation of a random excitation, rather than its expectation, costs at most √(2n) in amplitude for n equally loud independent directions. A mixture of alternatives (any prior over rooms and layouts) is no easier to detect than the prior’s average of its members, TV(P₀, Σ wᵢPᵢ) ≤ Σ wᵢ TV(P₀, Pᵢ): a bound that holds for every member holds for the mixture, while one hard member does not make a mixture hard (one hard pair defeats a uniform guarantee, not every prior). N images, where their independence is justified, raise the divergence N times, so the ceiling √(KL/2) grows at most √N times; the TV itself need not follow that ceiling.

E · depth

Two worlds a method cannot tell apart

For two admissible worlds at depths Δ apart, any depth estimate has equal-prior mean absolute error at least (Δ/2)(1 − TV). One indistinguishable pair is enough to rule out reliable depth over any class that contains it.

and the tight layer

What speckle adds

For speckle, motion shared by the scene leaves the image’s distribution unchanged, so only what differs from place to place counts: the Fisher information F = Σ|Φ̃(f − f′; f) − Φ̃(f − f′; f′)|², with a power-series remainder ε bounding the rest, ‖E‖ ≤ √F + ε (P2-25). This is far tighter than the certificate, and assumes the reference covariance is the identity, where the certificate proves its floor with the background in it.

Theorems A–E are from an independent derivation made for this lab and checked in its code (sim/sarsim/finite.py, with its tests); the tight layer is sim/sarsim/information.py. Background: Duchi’s lecture notes on total variation, data processing, Pinsker and two-point testing.

The question is broader than detection: presence, horizontal location, shape, relative vertical geometry and absolute depth are scored apart

Does one complex SAR acquisition hold reproducible information about what is actually underground, where it is or its shape, that survives controls and predicts withheld ground, even if its depth stays uncertain? A method that drew a branching tunnel or a room’s layout at one of two possible depths would be a major success, so an uncalibrated or aliased depth scale does not by itself dismiss a method. Each claim is judged on its own evidence.

In detail: each layer and check, folded

Telling one layout from another is bounded the same way, and moving a reconstruction up or down cannot rescue it

the calculation, in full

If two layouts leave nearly the same image in distribution, no algorithm can tell them apart, however it reconstructs. A uniformly reliable shape mapper must separate two layouts even after the depth freedom it is allowed; the closest admissible pair of depth variants is no easier than the pair at the same depth, so the same-depth bound holds for every allowance. Each layout’s imprint is the lab’s solver (statically for the ambient levels, dynamically for a lorry close by), and the bound is on the difference between two layouts’ imprints (P2-32).

Limits within a stated measurement and physical model

The theorems and the three layers above: finite, algorithm-independent, and scoped to fully developed speckle (or any texture, for the oracle), the Doppler-to-time relation, the lab’s linear elastic imprints and the regional ambient levels.

a research goal

Limits for a defined class of realistic archaeological settings

Feasible, with stronger physical validation: measured ground motion at the site during a pass, measured coupling over known voids, a qualified observation model and noise floor, and the open regime examined by experiment rather than by bound.

not claimed

That every conceivable single-image application has no archaeological value

Too broad for this work: mechanisms, environments and acquisitions outside the model are not covered, and the open regime is open. The outcome sought is this boundary, not a verdict fixed in advance.

What a mechanism must supply: a large enough displacement integral over the ground, whatever its pattern; smooth patterns need far more

the calculation, in full

Corrected on 30 September after two independent reviews. The solid curves were first described as the motion any mechanism must supply; they hold only for the smooth pattern computed. The requirement for every pattern is on the displacement integral E = ∫ maxt |Δulos|² dA (m⁴: an integrated squared displacement envelope, not a mechanical energy). Under strong shaking the whole-image certificate has no valid reference (the ground without a room already moves with the source), so its curve applies to quiet ground; the oracle’s applies under any shaking, and the benchmark above bounds a single image under a lorry line by line.

conditional

Phase modulation, nonlinear coupling, anything

The bounds use the full complex change of the image, phase included; no spectral peak is needed. Any mechanism, linear or not, reaches one image only through the motion it adds. An oracle told the reflectivity, the excitation and both models, with receiver noise at the best specified 18.4 dB per cell, cannot reach 95% at 5% while that motion’s displacement integral stays below 1.5 × 10⁻⁸ m⁴, whatever its pattern or time history, and whatever motion both worlds share, which cancels from its mean echoes; below 2.2 × 10⁻¹¹ m⁴ it stays within five points of chance. Every method told less inherits this. Under quiet ground the speckle certificate, with Giza’s background in the reference, gives 1.4 × 10⁻⁹ m⁴ without assuming receiver noise.

The pattern matters as well as the size. A Gaussian 4 m wide at 0.2 Hz, multiplied by a cosine of 4 m period along track, holds 39 times its information though nowhere larger (489 times for a 1 m period). The Gaussian’s information is 4.7 × 10⁻⁴ of the general upper bound for its envelope, 4N⟨Φ²⟩, which is a bound, not an attainable optimum; at 80 Hz it is 49%.

A change of phase alone moves the ground by at most twice the motion it acts on, 2a. To escape this exclusion, under its stated amplitude and noise, such a mechanism must change the phase over more than Emin/(2a)²: 858,457 m² under Giza’s regional microseisms, a disk of radius 523 m. That is necessary to escape this particular bound, not sufficient for detection. Whether a physically justified cavity-induced field, tails included, reaches the threshold is the table’s question below. A mechanism with gain is not excluded: it needs its output specified (source, footprint, amplitude and phase, losses, duration) before it can be tested.

An obstruction sought, and not found

Could shallower voids mimic a deeper one? Small rooms 15 m down, spread over the ground in any non-negative mix, were fitted to the surface mark of the bench room 30 m down on the lab’s solver (two solved together agree with their sum to 3.7%): 71% of the mark is left unmatched, and the bench room 15 m down alone leaves 78%. In uniform rock the mark changes shape with depth, not only size, so telling these depths apart takes only 1.4 times the signal detection takes. Theorem E's floor on any method's mean depth error, where detection just reaches 95% at 5%, is then only 1.8 m of their 15 m separation. Richer classes (layered rock, fractures, voids of other shapes) could still mimic one another; that is not tested.

the published method · P2-28

Its depth follows the shaking, not the room

Run whole over the bench room 15 m down, the same room 30 m down and a granite block, each imprint boosted to 2 rad, the published focusing spreads each change over its whole axis: 16% of the 15 m room’s change lies between 12 and 18 m and 16% of the 30 m room’s between 27 and 33 m, where 15% and 15% of the axis lie. Horizontally its change does gather over the room (2.6 times elsewhere), but a random perturbation of the same pixels gathers almost as much (2.4 times): in this paired comparison the picture changes where the image changes. Its depth coordinate is an uncalibrated focus coordinate, the frequency of the registration series (P2-06): a calibration and aliasing problem, which a method that recovered the right layout would survive. It does not recover the layout: in the shape test (above) its blind map names a layout no better than guessing even where a reference detector names every one.
