Proof · what any way of reading one image could learn, and what it assumes

The bound, finite and stated

Does one satellite radar image hold reproducible information about what is underground (whether something is there, where it is, its shape) even if its depth stays uncertain? Within a stated measurement model this can be bounded for every algorithm at once, for detection and for telling one layout from another, in three layers, each proved without a small-motion approximation. The aim is a defensible boundary: which conditions rule out useful recovery and which deserve experiments. The physical inputs, a structure’s imprint and the shaking’s level, are models and are marked as such.

A detection target of 95% found with 5% false alarms needs the two hypotheses, room and no room, to differ by a total variation of at least 0.9; every processor of the same measurements, tomograms and learned models included, inherits whatever total variation the measurements have. The three layers bound it differently. The certificate is simple and robust: it gives the room’s largest phase to every pixel of the whole image and proves a floor under the reference covariance with the ground’s background motion in it. The tight calculation uses the structure speckle imposes (motion shared by the scene holds nothing) and bounds the finite change with an explicit remainder. The oracle needs no model of the texture at all. All three agree that the ground’s own trembling leaves nothing useful; they differ by orders of magnitude in how little, which is what their different assumptions buy.

The question is broader than detection: presence, horizontal location, shape, relative vertical geometry and absolute depth are scored apart

Does one complex SAR acquisition hold reproducible information about what is actually underground, where it is or its shape, that survives controls and predicts withheld ground, even if its depth stays uncertain? A method that drew a branching tunnel or a room’s layout at one of two possible depths would be a major success, so an uncalibrated or aliased depth scale does not by itself dismiss a method. Each claim is judged on its own evidence.

A bound on detection rate − false-alarm rate is a bound at every operating point: found ≤ false alarms + TV. Under the regional microseism envelope that leaves no prospecting value within the model, even for ranking sites (TV ≤ 1.7 × 10⁻⁴); where TV is large, as in the open regime, a method could have prospecting value short of 95% at 5%, and it is not excluded.

Telling one layout from another is bounded the same way, and moving a reconstruction up or down cannot rescue it

If two layouts leave nearly the same image in distribution, no algorithm can tell them apart, however it reconstructs. A uniformly reliable shape mapper must separate two layouts even after the depth freedom it is allowed; the closest admissible pair of depth variants is no easier than the pair at the same depth, so the same-depth bound holds for every allowance. Each layout’s imprint is the lab’s solver (statically for the ambient levels, dynamically for a lorry close by), and the bound is on the difference between two layouts’ imprints (P2-32).

The shape test: where the data hold a layout, a reference detector names and places it; the published picture, read blind, does neither

Each layout’s imprint is the lab’s solver; the image is the synthesizer’s, with the unrelated ground and the shaking matched across layouts and controls (no cavity, and a motionless copy), three realisations each (P2-31). The reference detector is told the shaking: it names a layout by its statistic at the true place and places it by scanning every placement. The published method’s plan map is read blind; its change against the same ground without the cavity is a diagnostic it never has. Amplified levels are diagnostics, not predictions.

Limits within a stated measurement and physical model

The theorems and the three layers above: finite, algorithm-independent, and scoped to fully developed speckle (or any texture, for the oracle), the Doppler-to-time relation, the lab’s linear elastic imprints and the regional ambient levels.

a research goal

Limits for a defined class of realistic archaeological settings

Feasible, with stronger physical validation: measured ground motion at the site during a pass, measured coupling over known voids, a qualified observation model and noise floor, and the open regime examined by experiment rather than by bound.

not claimed

That every conceivable single-image application has no archaeological value

Too broad for this work: mechanisms, environments and acquisitions outside the model are not covered, and the open regime is open. The outcome sought is this boundary, not a verdict fixed in advance.

The certificate and the tight bound hold within fully developed speckle and the Doppler-to-time relation (checked pulse by pulse, P2-25); the oracle holds for any texture with independent phases, at 30 dB per resolution cell (above the brightest natural ground over ICEYE’s best Dwell noise floor, -26.7 dB). The ambient levels are regional, not upper limits at the pyramids. On small test images the best test comes within 1.4 times of the tight bound: these are bounds, not what a detector achieves.

Five theorems, each finite; none assumes a resonance peak or a particular algorithm

A · every processor

Processing cannot add distinguishability

For any test, detection rate − false-alarm rate ≤ TV(P₁, P₀), and for equal priors the best accuracy is ½ + TV/2. Any channel applied the same way under both hypotheses, a sub-aperture bank, a tomogram, a renderer, a trained network, contracts TV. So 95% found at 5% false alarms needs TV ≥ 0.9, and a proved TV below it excludes that target for every method at once.

Proof

Split p₁ − p₀ into positive and negative parts of equal mass TV. A test 0 ≤ t ≤ 1 integrates to at most TV against it; the indicator of the positive part attains it. A processor K pulls back to the test y ↦ E[t(K(y))], so it can do no better on K’s output.

B · the finite change

The exact Gaussian divergence, bounded

For proper complex Gaussians, KL = tr(C₀⁻¹C₁) − d − log det(C₀⁻¹C₁) + δm*C₀⁻¹δm. With E the whitened covariance change and its eigenvalues ej > −1, KL = Σ(ej − log(1 + ej)) + mean term, and since e − log(1 + e) ≤ e²/(2 min(1 + e, 1)), KL ≤ ‖E‖F²/(2(1 − ‖E‖)). Pinsker and Bretagnolle–Huber turn KL into TV. No expansion in the displacement is made.

C · a certificate from a phase envelope

The whole image given the room’s largest phase

If ‖C₀−½ΔA‖F ≤ q, ρ = 2q + q² bounds ‖E‖ exactly (E = DB* + BD* + DD*), so TV ≤ ρ/(2(1 − ρ)). A phase-only change obeys |e−iΦ − 1|² ≤ Φ², so q² ≤ Σ|T₀|²Φ² / λmin(C₀) ≤ m P²/λmin: the peak phase P at every one of the image’s 1.31 billion pixels, over its 703 million retained bins. A background motion bounded the same way proves λmin(C₀) ≥ (1 − √Qbg)² = 0.0559.

D · the oracle

Told everything but the answer

Given the reflectivity, the background motion, the shaking and both models, the raw echoes are Gaussian with the receiver noise’s covariance and means that differ by the echo of the room-specific motion alone: TV = erf(√d/2) exactly, d the noise-normalised energy of that difference. Averaged over what the oracle was told (only independent uniform phases needed), TV ≤ erf(√(E d)/2); the background motion cancels; 95% at 5% needs E d ≥ 5.41. Checked against pulse-by-pulse echoes on the real dwell: 0.993 ± 0.025 of the formula.

E · depth

Two worlds a method cannot tell apart

For two admissible worlds at depths Δ apart, any depth estimate has equal-prior mean absolute error at least (Δ/2)(1 − TV). One indistinguishable pair is enough to rule out reliable depth over any class that contains it.

and the tight layer

What speckle adds

For speckle, motion shared by the scene leaves the image’s distribution unchanged, so only what differs from place to place counts: the Fisher information F = Σ|Φ̃(f − f′; f) − Φ̃(f − f′; f′)|², with a power-series remainder ε bounding the rest, ‖E‖ ≤ √F + ε (P2-25). This is far tighter than the certificate, and assumes the reference covariance is the identity, where the certificate proves its floor with the background in it.

Theorems A–E are from an independent derivation made for this lab and checked in its code (sim/sarsim/finite.py, with its tests); the tight layer is sim/sarsim/information.py. Background: Duchi’s lecture notes on total variation, data processing, Pinsker and two-point testing.

What a mechanism must supply: the motion it adds to the ground over the room, whatever makes it

conditional

Phase modulation, nonlinear coupling, anything

The bounds use the full complex change of the image, phase included; no spectral peak is needed. Any mechanism, linear or not, reaches one image only through the motion it adds over the room, and the curve says how much. A change of phase alone moves the ground by at most twice the motion it acts on: within the background envelope of 72 nm, an arbitrary phase modulation on 52,600 pixels (about 1,000 m²) is capped at 9.55% found at 5% (the certificate), and at Giza’s regional levels falls short of the exact curve 2.1 × 10⁴ times; over the whole image the certificate does not decide. A mechanism with gain, gathering energy over the room for the whole pass, is not excluded: it needs its output specified (source, footprint, amplitude and phase, losses, duration) before it can be tested.

Try an envelope in the certificate

The loose certificate (Theorem C): zero-mean Gaussian texture, phase-only change, continuum area. Inputs are assumptions, not measured motion.

Depth is carried by the mark’s shape in this model: rooms spread 15 m down leave 71% of a deeper room’s mark unmatched; the published method’s axis does not use it

any method · P2-29, P2-30

An obstruction sought, and not found

Could shallower voids mimic a deeper one? Small rooms 15 m down, spread over the ground in any non-negative mix, were fitted to the surface mark of the bench room 30 m down on the lab’s solver (two solved together agree with their sum to 3.7%): 71% of the mark is left unmatched, and the bench room 15 m down alone leaves 78%. In uniform rock the mark changes shape with depth, not only size, so telling these depths apart takes only 1.4 times the signal detection takes. Theorem E's floor on any method's mean depth error, where detection just reaches 95% at 5%, is then only 1.8 m of their 15 m separation. Richer classes (layered rock, fractures, voids of other shapes) could still mimic one another; that is not tested.

the published method · P2-28

Its depth follows the shaking, not the room

Run whole over the bench room 15 m down, the same room 30 m down and a granite block, each imprint boosted to 2 rad, the published focusing spreads each change over its whole axis: 16% of the 15 m room’s change lies between 12 and 18 m and 16% of the 30 m room’s between 27 and 33 m, where 15% and 15% of the axis lie. Horizontally its change does gather over the room (2.6 times elsewhere), but a random perturbation of the same pixels gathers almost as much (2.4 times): in this paired comparison the picture changes where the image changes. Its depth coordinate is an uncalibrated focus coordinate, the frequency of the registration series (P2-06): a calibration and aliasing problem, which a method that recovered the right layout would survive. It does not recover the layout: in the shape test (above) its blind map names a layout no better than guessing even where a reference detector names every one.

A method that succeeds on well-designed, withheld ground would show that the measurements or these inputs deserve revision; the bounds are written so such a revision can be made, not to protect a conclusion. The test that would settle it is frozen and blind: motion recovered from a measured moving reflector first, then withheld cavities and controls predicted with settings fixed in advance (What would settle it). What would help most from the method’s proponents is a predicted room-specific surface motion, with its source, footprint, amplitude and phase, losses and duration, which can be put straight into these formulas without releasing any software.
