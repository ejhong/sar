The satellite over the same ground

Phase 2 simulates the real ICEYE dwell over Giza, pixel by pixel, over ground whose chambers and motion are known, and checks it on the real image. The answer is no, for three reasons that do not depend on one another: one image cannot see ordinary ground move, a chamber barely changes how the ground moves, and the published depth is only the frequency of the motion.

Each is sufficient alone. The first is about the estimator: lookslook radarAn image formed from part of the Doppler band. In a dwell the band is time, so a look sees the ground as it was during a few seconds, and averages any motion over them. cut from one image share their spectrum sample for sample, so correlating them cannot see motion. The second is about physics: ambient waves are kilometres long and strain the rock around a chamber almost uniformly. The third is about the method’s algebra: its steering grows with time, so its focusing is a Fourier transform over time. The measured record from the real acquisitions (the first investigation) agrees, and three of its readings are corrected below.

Looks cut from one image cannot see ordinary ground move

Every single-image method, published or ours, reads motion by registering one image’s looks against each other. Two such looks share the spectrum they overlap, sample for sample, so their cross-spectrum is real and non-negative and their correlation locks at zero, whatever the ground does. For natural ground, whose specklespeckle radarThe grainy texture of radar images of natural ground: many small scatterers in each cell interfering. Each Doppler bin holds an independent speckle pattern. is independent in every Doppler bin, motion shared by a patch leaves the image’s statistics exactly unchanged. Here a 20 m stretch of the real Giza image is made to sway at 2 mm/s, and read three ways.

A chamber changes the ground’s own motion by less than a ten-thousandth of it

Whatever a satellite could use to find a chamber passively would have to be its imprintimprint seismic · radarThe difference a buried chamber makes to how the surface moves as a wave passes: what any passive method would have to detect.: the difference it makes to how the surface moves. Ambient waves are far longer than the chamber and its depth, so the rock around it is strained almost uniformly and almost statically. The lab’s solver answers the static question directly: load the rock with a uniform horizontal stress, open the chamber, and let the ground settle.

Allowing every advantage, one dwell falls short by ten thousand to a hundred million

The smallest imprint a dwell could detect, from what P2-03 measured: every one of its independent looks counted as a full measurement, a 3-sigma threshold, the look’s gain at the imprint’s frequency, and the best tracker for each target. Under Giza’s microseisms that is 900 µm/s on the plateau’s own texture, 62 µm/s on a bright scatterer directly above the chamber, and 6 µm/s on a corner reflector that no archive image contains, against an imprint of 3.2 × 10−6 µm/s. For the plateau the ground would have to shake at 14 m/s, beyond the strongest earthquakes recorded, for the whole dwell.

The published depth is the frequency of the motion: 6.76 m per hertz

The published focusing projects each patch’s trajectory on a steering wave whose wavenumber grows with the platform’s offset, and on a dwell the offset grows steadily with time. So the focusing is a Fourier transform over time, and motion at frequency f appears at a depth of f times λs R sin θ / 2Vs, whatever is under the ground. Given the exact motion at every pair, with none of the blindness above, it still cannot say where a chamber is.

What Phase 2 corrects in the first investigation, and what still stands

R9 · corrected

The coherence curve measured the looks, not the ground

Its 0.98 s to half and 1.76 s to a tenth are 1 − Δ/W for its 1.96 s looks, reproduced by simulated desert with no decorrelation and by the real crop’s own spectrum. How stable the ground is across a dwell has not been measured.

R4 · corrected

The 77 µm/s floor is noise, not sensitivity

It is the scatter of a complex registration that recovers 1% of real ground’s motion. On bright point targets a magnitude tracker does follow motion, with a scatter of the same order.

R7 · corrected

The injected motion never came back

Its recovery gains sit near zero at every amplitude up to 1 cm, as expected of motion common to a whole crop of speckle. Its later frequencies, 1, 2 and 3 Hz, sit on the nulls of its looks.

R10 · corrected

Bright pixels were graded with the same identity

Complex coherence between one image’s looks is set by the spectrum whatever the pixels are. A lone bright scatterer’s envelope in fact follows its motion across the dwell.

R13 · stands

Hundreds of surveyed burial shafts give no signal at their depths

Over the Eastern and Western Cemeteries, shafts 5 to 30 m deep, the depth profiles match bare plateau, before and after matching surface brightness.

R2, R15, R16 · stand

The depth axis is set by the processing, and resolution by the look

The published axis folds within metres and repeats by an algebraic identity (P2-06 gives it a frequency); a look short enough to follow a wave through the ground resolves tens of metres at best.
