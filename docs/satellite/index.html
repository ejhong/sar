The satellite over the same ground

Phase 2 flies an ICEYE Spotlight Dwell, with the real Giza and Sacsayhuamán parameters, over the same simulated shaking ground the geophones recorded. What the radar can measure is then compared pixel by pixel with what the seismometers measured, and handed to the same inversions.

The radar sees only the surface, so it is judged on the surface first

X-bandX-band radarRadar near 9.6 GHz (3.1 cm wavelength). It enters dry limestone by centimetres, not metres. enters centimetres of dry limestone, so any radar view of the underworld must come through motion of the surface. Phase 2 simulates exactly that motion and asks, in order, three questions of increasing difficulty.

step 1

Does the radar record the ground's motion?

The Phase 1 surface wavefield, projected on the real line of sight, modulates a synthesised dwell; its velocity series is compared with the geophone at the same spot.

step 2

Does the published pipeline find the chambers?

The same simulated product goes through the Doppler-tomography chain exactly as published, over ground whose chambers are known.

step 3

Can any inversion make a tomogram from it?

The radar's velocity field, with its real sampling, record length and floor, is given to the Phase 1 inversions as if it were a geophone array.

What the real acquisitions already show

From the first investigation, measured on the real products, not simulated. Each becomes a calibration or a check for the Phase 2 simulation. The full record

R9 · measured

Time diversity and coherence are the same axis in a dwell

Coherence with the band-centre look halves by 0.98 s of separation and falls to a tenth by 1.76 s, on the pyramid and on open desert alike.

R4 · measured

One dwell measures a two-second velocity series with a 77 µm/s floor

Looks kept inside the coherent window give a line-of-sight velocity series for every pixel. Its floor on the brightest targets is the same on monuments and desert.

R13 · measured

Hundreds of surveyed burial shafts give no signal at their depths

Over the Eastern and Western Cemeteries, shafts 5 to 30 m deep, the depth profiles match bare plateau: standardised difference +0.022, and +0.031 after matching surface brightness.

R2 · measured

The depth axis is set by the processing, not by the ground

The platform moves 168 km during the aperture, so a bank over the whole aperture folds depth back at 6.8 m and repeats it at 13.6 m. Hundreds of metres need about one percent of the aperture.

R15 · measured

The modal gate picks which pixels get a depth, not which depth is right

The steering model repeats exactly every 27.4 m by an algebraic identity. The gate itself separates Khafre from plateau no better than chance (AUC 0.504).

R11 · measured

Used as a dense array, the image holds no propagating wave

Cross-correlating 759 pixel series over 1.8 km finds a median peak of 0.004, against 0.49 for an injected wavefield put through the identical stack.
