Method and sources

How what we found is known, how sure each part is, and how to check it. Everything on this site is generated from the repository by code anyone can run: the compositions from their JSON, the simulations from their scripts, the figures and volumes from the runs that made them. Nothing is edited by hand, and every number carries its source or is marked as assumed.

Three reasons, each checked against something it could have failed, and each with its own limits

P2-03 · real and simulated

Correlating one image’s looks registers little of ground motion

Looks cut from one image share their overlapping spectrum; for whole images their correlation peaks at zero lag exactly, and for patch registration that is a strong pull toward zero, measured rather than proved. For speckle, motion shared by a patch leaves the image’s distribution unchanged.

Checked: the same block motion is recovered in full against a motionless twin, so the motion is in the data; a lone bright point’s envelope follows its motion (100 ± 2%); the real crop’s spectrum reproduces its own coherence to 10−9. Limit: measured for this estimator; others need their own measurement.

P2-04, P2-05 · modelled

In our model, the chamber’s imprint is far below the reach

Static relaxation with the lab’s elastic solver gives the chamber’s imprint on the ground’s motion (6.6 × 10−5 of it); the budget combines it with what P2-03 measured, allowing every advantage.

Checked: intact rock stays exactly still; the result changes 3.5% from a 1 m to a 0.5 m grid and not at all with domain size; Eshelby’s void with Okada’s point sources agrees within 40%; the detection threshold is confirmed by simulation (24 of 24 detected). Limit: one small chamber in uniform rock, quasi-static, at the regional microseism level; not a measurement at Giza during a pass.

P2-06, P2-07 · algebra, checked in code

The depth axis is a frequency axis

The steering wavenumber grows linearly with slow time, so the published focusing is a Fourier transform over time: 6.76 m of depth per hertz on the real geometry, whatever lies below.

Checked: the pipeline’s own code reproduces the rule to the grid, folds where predicted, and draws identical profiles over a chamber at 15 m, at 30 m and over rock; run whole (P2-07), its sections with and without the chamber differ by 10−8. Limit: the scale depends on the sound wavelength, which rescales it without changing the fit (a calibration ambiguity); the relation makes absolute depth uncalibrated, and does not by itself decide whether horizontal location or shape survive.

Graded claim by claim on the satellite page, which also says what would settle what is open. The full audit, with what is not established and the corrections adopted after an independent review, is METHOD_AUDIT.md; every run’s parameters, code and data are linked from its details panel on the satellite page.

Two communities, one vocabulary

Seismologists and radar engineers rarely read each other. The terms this site uses from each, in plain words; they are underlined where they appear.

ambient-noise interferometry seismic
Cross-correlating the background hum recorded at two sensors turns it into the wave that would travel from one to the other, so noise can stand in for a source.
azimuth radar
The direction along the satellite’s track, as opposed to range, the direction of the beam.
coherence radar
How similar two radar looks of the same ground are, from 0 to 1. Phase can only be compared where coherence is high.
crosshole seismic
A survey with sources down one borehole and receivers down another, so rays pass through the ground between them rather than skimming the surface.
Doppler frequency radar
The frequency shift of an echo from the satellite’s motion. In a focused image it maps to the time and direction from which a target was seen.
Doppler tomography radar
The claimed method: reading ground vibration from the differences between sub-apertures of one image and focusing it into depth. Published 2022, retracted 2026.
eikonal equation seismic
The high-frequency limit of the wave equation: it gives the earliest arrival time everywhere, and the rays are its steepest descent.
first break seismic
The first arrival of energy on a seismic record. Its timing, picked on every record, is the data of travel-time tomography.
Fisher information seismic · radar
How sharply the probability of the data changes with a parameter. Its inverse bounds the variance of any unbiased estimate (the Cramér–Rao bound), and for a small change it sets, to first order, how well any test can tell the two cases apart; for a finite change it bounds that with an explicit remainder.
full-waveform inversion seismic
Fitting whole seismic records, not just arrival times, by adjusting a model of the ground; each step is a gradient computed by propagating the misfit backwards in time.
geophone seismic
A ground-motion sensor recording velocity; three-component geophones record east, north and up.
imprint seismic · radar
The difference a buried chamber makes to how the surface moves as a wave passes: what any passive method would have to detect.
line of sight radar
The direction from the satellite to the ground; radar measures motion only along it.
look radar
An image formed from part of the Doppler band. In a dwell the band is time, so a look sees the ground as it was during a few seconds, and averages any motion over them.
microseism seismic
The ground’s constant hum from ocean swell, strongest between 0.1 and 0.3 Hz, felt everywhere on Earth as surface waves kilometres long.
P wave seismic
The compressional wave: the fastest, so the first to arrive. About 3,300 m/s in Giza limestone.
PML seismic
Perfectly matched layer: an absorbing boundary that lets simulated waves leave the grid without reflecting.
points per wavelength seismic
How many grid cells span the shortest wave simulated; too few and the waves travel at the wrong speed.
Rayleigh wave seismic
A wave that rolls along the ground surface, slightly slower than S, carrying most of a hammer blow’s energy. It samples to about a third of its wavelength in depth.
reverse-time migration seismic
Imaging by sending recorded waves backwards through a model of the ground and marking where they meet the waves sent forward from the source. It is the first step of full-waveform inversion.
S wave seismic
The shear wave: slower than P (about 1,800 m/s in the same rock) and unable to cross air or water.
SLC radar
Single-look complex image: a focused radar image that keeps amplitude and phase for every pixel.
speckle radar
The grainy texture of radar images of natural ground: many small scatterers in each cell interfering. Each Doppler bin holds an independent speckle pattern.
Spotlight Dwell radar
An ICEYE mode that keeps the beam on one scene for tens of seconds, here 24.5 s, far longer than a normal image needs.
sub-aperture radar
A part of the synthetic aperture processed on its own: a look from a slightly different direction and time.
synthetic aperture radar
The stretch of orbit over which echoes are combined into one image; a long aperture gives fine resolution along the track.
tomogram seismic · radar
A three-dimensional image of a property (here, wave speed) reconstructed from measurements taken around it.
travel-time tomography seismic
Finding the speed of the ground from how long first arrivals take between many sources and receivers, by tracing their rays and adjusting the speeds until the times fit.
virtual source seismic
A sensor made to act as a source: the correlation of its noise record with another sensor’s is the wave a real source there would have sent. Noise sources spread over the ground give it as minus the lag derivative of the correlation.
X-band radar
Radar near 9.6 GHz (3.1 cm wavelength). It enters dry limestone by centimetres, not metres.

Three commands rebuild everything

cd sim && uv sync && uv run pytest                     # the simulation and its checks (~40 s)
uv run python experiments/p2_07_whole_chain.py         # any run, e.g. the whole chain (~5 min)
uv run python -m katabasis.export                      # everything the site reads
cd ../web && npm ci && npm run build                   # the site, into docs/

The lab’s Python package keeps its first name, katabasis. Real-image steps need the ICEYE products, which are not in the repository; their acquisition records are, so everything else reruns anywhere.

One command runs a method over any area of a product and adds the result to the lab

cd sim
uv run --with scikit-image python -m katabasis.lab gated --name khafre --site giza --centre 0,0 --twin 101
uv run python -m katabasis.export                      # the run appears in the lab, under the satellite

gated runs the stricter gated reconstruction, the publicReplication and Verification of the Biondi Protocol (v1.8 standalone package, commit ac6f2500), unchanged but for its input paths, along east–west lines --step metres apart across a square of the site (--half-ew, --half-ns), each line placed through the product’s RPC over the site’s surface (--lines ns lays them north–south). Its fit scores are hung below the surface into a volume, unsmoothed and empty where nothing passed; --twin SEED runs the same raster on a motionless copy of the image, the control every picture needs, and --producttakes another pass, or a synthetic product. In the lab, every place a method was run shows at once, and the pass and the lines become choices: things a real feature should survive. Each run writes sim/results/lab_<name>/: its record (date, commit, the method’s source hash, every parameter) and its volumes, which the export adds to the lab with the record attached. More methods will join it as they are built.
